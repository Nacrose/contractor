## Permission and transaction contracts

- VAT register reads are deliberately composite. Purchase data requires project VAT, materials, subcontractors, equipment, equipment financials, payments and accounting view grants. Sales requires VAT and IPC view; Schedule 10 requires the purchase-side grants plus IPC view. Direct VAT bill creation requires both `project.vat.edit` and `project.accounting.edit`, a fiscal year that is not locked, applicable delegation limits and valid Nepal PAN where supplied. It uses decimal helpers, serializes duplicate bill keys, writes the bill and any journal entry in a tenant transaction, and audits the result. Scanned-bill attachment authority is checked against the target record type, such as material receipt, IPC, subcontractor bill or standalone VAT bill.
- Requisition creation requires purchase-order edit plus materials and vendor view. Every line must reference a material in the project, vendors must be in that project or organization, and each line has at least three quotes. Choosing a quote above the lowest total requires a written justification. The transaction allocates a sequence number and creates the requisition in `pending_approval` with its quote rows. Approval and rejection require the matching `project.purchaseRequisitions.approve` or `.reject` action permission as well as purchase-order edit; both are guarded state transitions and rejection requires a reason.
- Purchase-order generation requires purchase-order edit plus materials/vendor view. It accepts only approved or partially ordered requisition rows, groups them by selected vendor, uses the selected quote’s ex-factory plus transport rates, and caps each generated quantity at the fresh remaining quantity. It row-locks requisition items and rechecks them in the transaction so concurrent generations cannot over-order; it then updates each requisition to approved, partially ordered or ordered based on remaining quantities.
- A direct PO requires `project.purchaseOrders.issue`, a permitted role, a reason and at least one project material/vendor. It creates an issued PO and commitment cost directly. Other PO issuance/receipt/cancellation transitions require their specific action permission; issue/cancel also require accounting edit, while receiving requires materials edit. Receiving creates stock transactions and updates received quantities in the same transaction. Cancellation removes the commitment and returns uncovered requisition quantities to an orderable state. Draft deletion has its own permission.
- WF-015's `VendorBillsTab` is another unreferenced materials component (24 UI event candidates). It owns the received-but-unbilled PO worklist, vendor bill creation, duplicate checks, three-way-match data and bill payment entry, but no mapped route imports it. The server requires each bill to reference a PO and received GRN value, serializes bill registration on the PO row, enforces fiscal locks and a cumulative received-value cap, and writes the bill and its accounting effects transactionally. Payment uses separate disbursement authority, an idempotency key and a guarded settlement update. Contractor must expose the bill worklist and payment flow deliberately; the existence of these APIs does not make them navigable.
- Material transactions require materials edit and, for linked entities, view authority over the PO or subcontractor. A receipt requires a linked issued/partially-received PO; the material and PO must belong to the project. Receipt quantities cannot exceed the ordered balance; a GRN rate deviation above 2% requires an explicit override and reason. Stock adjustments require a reason, damage/theft requires remarks, and large non-opening adjustments become pending reconciliation work instead of directly changing stock. Gate entries are created pending and stock is posted only by a separate receiving transaction.
- Inter-site transfer requires materials edit on both projects, same-organization containment, distinct origin/destination, valid project-owned stores, and sufficient origin/store stock. Stock decrements are guarded atomically inside a transaction to prevent negative stock. Instant transfers create the destination material/receipt and paired internal cost entries; non-instant transfers remain in transit for receipt or cancellation. A missing rate falls back to last known inbound rate or zero; it never invents a value from the reorder threshold.

For WF-017, the mounted inventory route reaches `InterSiteTransferDialog`: its form submits `interSiteTransfer.transferMaterial`, with a switch between instant receipt and a two-step in-transit dispatch. The 17 reachable dialog event candidates cover project/store/material selection, quantity and rate inputs, logistics fields, mode selection, submit, dismissal and success callbacks; they are individually marked source-reviewed in the crosswalk. The mapped routes do not reach `InterSiteTransfersTab`, which owns the transfer list and cancel mutation, or `TransferAcknowledgementDialog`, which owns GRN receipt. The canonical source therefore contains list, receive and cancel controls, but those components are disconnected from the indexed route/layout graph and do not complete a navigable user journey. Their event candidates are retained in `original-workflow-source-crosswalk.json` under `unreferencedUiComponentsWithRelatedHooks`; their presence is a source parity gap, not proof of runtime availability.

For WF-016, none of the mapped inventory, materials or stock-alert routes imports `StoreLocationsTab`. Its orphaned component contains store listing, store creation, in-charge search, and inter-store stock transfer (22 event candidates). The server `storeLocation.create` endpoint requires `project.materials.edit`, rechecks permission inside the tenant transaction, validates the selected in-charge, updates the project's default-store flag atomically, and audits creation. `storeLocation.transferStock` requires the same permission, verifies that material and both stores belong to the project and are active, checks source balance, then guards an atomic stock decrement and destination upsert in one transaction while writing a transfer ledger row and audit record. These server operations exist, but the canonical UI route graph does not expose store creation or intra-project store transfer. The crosswalk assigns this component to WF-016 only; its shared `material.list` selector must not make it appear to be a purchase-requisition or field-capture screen.

Two more WF-016 screens are disconnected. `/inventory` mounts `OrgMaterialsTabs` and `ProjectMaterialsWorkspace`; it does not reach `OrgInventoryTab` (the organization-wide `material.listOrgInventory` matrix) or `ReconciliationReport` (the physical-count/reconciliation history and decisions). The reconciliation server path creates a pending count without directly changing stock; approval rechecks the count under a row lock, requires the stock-approval permission plus materials view and accounting edit, rejects self-approval, honors fiscal locks, and posts the signed stock transaction and journal entry in the same transaction. Rejection is separately permissioned and requires a reason. These are implemented server contracts with no mapped screen in the canonical UI graph, so organization stock aggregation and physical-count review remain navigation/parity requirements.

The reachable `InwardDeliveryDialog` on `ROUTE-034` adds 36 reviewed gate/receipt controls. It supports a pending gate pass with vehicle, driver, challan, estimated quantity and scan; then either assigns accepted quantities to issued/partially received PO lines or creates a reasoned Direct PO before receipt. Challan, damaged and accepted quantities are collected separately, and a weighbridge helper can fill the estimated quantity. The successful GRN path updates stock, store valuation, transaction rows, PO received quantities and PO lifecycle within `material.createBulkReceive`'s tenant transaction; the gate entry is marked received in that transaction. The server rejects foreign-project references, unissued POs, over-receipts and rate variance above 2% without an override reason. The dialog does not submit the override/reason fields, so a user can edit a variance rate but cannot satisfy that server branch. The Direct mode is a multi-step sequence (create gate, create Direct PO, receive GRN) across separate mutations, so a later failure can leave a pending gate and/or issued PO for recovery. Contractor should expose those intermediate states and a retry-safe resume path, and provide a reasoned rate-override control before describing the receipt as complete.

The route's `LogTransactionDialog` contributes another 24 controls, mapped to `material.createTransaction`. It handles single-line receive, issue, transfer and adjustment actions, with optional gate/PO and store links, payment classification, subcontractor deductions, emergency substitution, VAT/TDS details and an explicit adjustment reason. The server requires materials edit, appropriate PO/subcontractor view grants and an unlocked current date; it project-pins linked records, requires an issued PO for receipts, guards over-receipts and applies the 2% rate-variance override with an audited reason. It updates stock, store valuation, transaction rows and PO receipt state in a tenant transaction, with an advisory PO lock for concurrent receipts. Adjustments require a reason, require remarks for damage/theft, and corrections at or above NPR 25,000 become pending reconciliation requests with stock unchanged until approval. The dialog maps its override checkbox and remarks into the server's override/reason inputs. These controls should remain online-only, and a pending large adjustment must remain visibly distinct from posted stock.

The 27 reviewed `MaterialsInventoryTab` controls are the materials register's project/store/stock filters, table density, row transaction inspection and full-history handoffs, plus action-menu callbacks into the already distinct quick-buy, transfer, receive/issue, gate, requisition, PO, material and store dialogs. Row inspection opens the transaction detail surface; full history opens the material ledger, where a gate verification callback is separately routed. These register controls navigate or filter; they do not themselves post stock. The target dialogs and server guards remain the authority for creation, transfer and receipt.

No operation in these workflows is in the field offline outbox or exact durable-read whitelist. These are online actions. M00 names VAT, requisition, PO, material and transfer engine/tests in the canonical tree; file presence in the source crosswalk is not proof that the tests were executed or passed.

## Contractor implications and remaining review

T19–T20 must preserve the quote comparison, approval/rejection, partial ordering and receipt trail. Keep purchase orders, committed cost, received stock, bills and VAT records tied together; a requisition is not a PO, a gate entry is not a receipt, and a receipt is not a paid/approved invoice. Inventory transfers must prove both project grants and organization scope and preserve both ledgers. Tax totals must reconcile to their source rows and journal entries under locked fiscal-period and delegated-amount rules.

This document reviews server contracts, not every source trigger or complete register lifecycle. Product UI review, sample-ledger reconciliation, end-to-end transaction tests and deployed-backend compatibility remain open under T01/T04.

## WF-013 VAT register and tax summary control review

The 46 tax-summary controls on `ROUTE-046` and the same 46 on the Finance route's `ROUTE-074` are now source-reviewed and recorded on WF-013 in the crosswalk. They cover tab changes, register filters and search, scan previews, direct-bill creation, scan upload, Excel exports, date ranges, Schedule 10 PDF/workbook export, dialog dismissal and success refreshes.

Schedule 8 merges project material receipts, subcontractor bills, equipment hire, site expenses and direct VAT bills; its server query requires VAT, materials, subcontractor, equipment/financial, payment and accounting view. Schedule 9 merges IPC sales with direct sales and requires VAT plus IPC view. Schedule 10 reads both sides with the additional IPC grant. Register period and entity filters flow to the service; search and missing-scan filtering are applied to the loaded rows, and Excel exports only the currently filtered client rows. The backend caps merged registers at 5,000 rows and signals truncation, so Contractor must expose that signal and avoid presenting a partial export as a complete statutory ledger. Schedule 10's date period lives in local component state, while each register's filters use `useRegister`; parity requires the same period to feed the return and both supporting schedules.

The direct VAT dialog creates purchase, sales, expense, capital-goods or import bills, calculates VAT/TDS, accepts an optional PAN and inline scan, then writes the bill and journal entry in one tenant transaction. The service requires VAT and accounting edit, an unlocked fiscal date, delegation, PAN validation and duplicate protection. Its online mutation is audited. The dialog fixes the category to `office_overhead`; the server journals `capital_goods` to equipment but all other non-sales types, including import/expense, to the generic purchases account. The UI preview uses 13% when `vatPercent` is deliberately set to zero even though the server accepts zero. Manual entries can include TDS, but the posted journal creates TDS payable without recording a cash settlement. These classification and preview semantics need explicit acceptance and reconciled fixtures.

Missing-scan attachment can target purchase, sales, IPC, subcontractor, material-receipt and equipment-hire rows. The server checks edit permission for the selected source and updates only a row in the project under a tenant transaction. The screen gates every target with the generic VAT `canWrite` flag, so users may see an attach action that the source-specific server check will reject. The picker reads a data URL into component memory (10 MB client cap); the mutation permits inline PDFs and raster scans up to a 10 MB URL string, leaving a smaller practical file limit after base64 encoding. The upload handler omits the original filename, and the server uses a generic fallback. These paths have no durable client draft or offline retry.

`vatRegister.actionAccess` itself first requires purchase-register view before it computes separate purchase, sales and return visibility, and the workspace also issues purchase and sales queries regardless of the selected tab. This can surface permission errors to a user who should only see one register. Contractor should make each register's visibility independent, retain target-specific write checks, show truncation, preserve scan names and reconcile generated schedules against source rows and journals. All reviewed writes are online-only; source inspection does not qualify the deployed backend or browser behavior.

## WF-014 purchase requisition form and decision handoff

The 10 create-dialog controls and four requisition-detail handoff controls on `ROUTE-034` are source-reviewed and recorded on WF-014. The form chooses one material and quantity, gathers three distinct supplier quotes with ex-factory and transport rates, lets the user choose a supplier, requires a reason when that supplier is not the lowest total, adds remarks, and submits an online requisition. A budget variance query displays planned, procured and remaining quantity; it is advisory and does not block an over-budget request. Stock-alert prefill is read from per-project session storage and cleared after mount, so a failed create does not preserve a durable draft.

`requisition.create` rechecks project/material/vendor scope, at least three quotes and the higher-price justification, then allocates a sequence number and creates a `pending_approval` record plus its quotes in one tenant transaction. Approval and rejection are separate state transitions with distinct permissions; rejection requires a reason. The detail component wires those operations through the shared `WorkflowActions` confirmation/reason surface and invalidates detail/list reads after success. Those dynamic decision buttons are not emitted as individual trigger candidates by the static trigger index, so their control-to-handler mapping remains weaker than the indexed form and dialog handoff paths. Generating POs is another explicit downstream step: the detail screen opens `GeneratePODialog`, groups selected quantities by vendor, and refreshes requisition and material state after success. Do not collapse requisition submission, approval and PO issuance into one app action.

## WF-015 received-but-unbilled vendor bills (unreferenced UI)

The 24 indexed controls in `VendorBillsTab` (`ORPHAN-UI-E377890E42E5`) cover the status filter, PO billable worklist, bill creation form, duplicate/three-way-match lookups and vendor-payment form. The component is not imported by the mapped materials route or shared layout, so the original source contains this workflow but does not establish that a user can navigate to it.

Bill creation requires a linked PO, partner, invoice number and positive gross value. The component reads received-but-unbilled POs, shows PO/GRN match data, checks duplicate invoice numbers as the user types, calculates VAT/TDS, then submits `vendorBill.create`. The service requires payment edit plus vendor, PO and materials view, purchase-bill delegation and an unlocked date. Inside one tenant transaction it verifies the vendor and PO belong to the project, confirms received GRN value, limits cumulative bill value to the received amount plus the source's 10% tolerance, rejects duplicate vendor invoice numbers, creates the liability journal, and records a financial event. The bill and its ledger entry commit together.

The payment dialog defaults to the bill's remaining net payable and submits amount, method, date, reference and remarks to `vendorBill.recordPayment`. The service separately requires payment-disbursement authority, checks fiscal lock and overpayment in the transaction, uses an idempotency wrapper, records the payment, settles the bill, writes the journal and financial event, and optionally decrements a linked bank account. The UI does not offer or submit `bankAccountId`, so this screen cannot select an account for that balance decrement. It also does not pass an idempotency key, attach the source file supported by the bill schema, or offer a disputed status filter despite the server supporting disputed bills. Contractor should either expose this worklist with a complete bank/account and attachment path or keep it explicitly unavailable; the code's presence alone does not satisfy navigation or lifecycle parity.

The reachable `MaterialsProcurementTab`, `CreatePODialog` and `GeneratePODialog` contribute 41 more reviewed controls on `ROUTE-034`. The procurement register filters document type, status and project; opens requisition detail or PO inspection/printing; starts a PO or GRN fast-track; and offers draft deletion behind a confirmation. PO creation can auto-fill from an approved requisition or build line items directly. Direct PO mode requires a reason in the UI; the server separately enforces its direct-issue permission, role, accounting authority and reason, while standard creation validates project materials/vendors and updates requisition progress. Requisition-based PO generation supports automatic or manually selected quantities; client values are capped by remaining quantity, and the server row-locks/revalidates remaining balances inside its transaction. Deletion is a separate draft-only operation. The visible register and dialogs therefore preserve distinct requisition, approved quantity, issued PO, received GRN and bill states. The 41 IDs are listed as reviewed on WF-015 in the crosswalk; the vendor-bill component's 24 IDs remain separately marked as unreferenced.
